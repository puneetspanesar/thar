Where agents belong in a marketing team

A field report from a year of running AI agents on live marketing work: what they do well, where they fail and the rules we now follow.

We have spent the past year running AI agents on real client work: research, first drafts, reporting, outreach and a long list of small jobs nobody enjoys. Some of it worked better than we expected. Some of it failed in ways that would have embarrassed a client if a person had not caught it.

Where agents earn their keep

  • Research. Scanning search results, competitor sites, ads and reviews, then summarising what changed this week.
  • First drafts. Posts, ad variations, email sequences and outlines, written to a clear brief and brand guide.
  • Repurposing. Turning one interview, report or webinar into a month of content across formats.
  • Reporting. Pulling numbers from several tools into one weekly summary with the changes highlighted.
  • List building. Finding and enriching target accounts and contacts against a precise profile.

Where they still need a person

Agents are confident when they should not be. They will state a statistic that does not exist, flatten a brand voice into something generic, or miss that a perfectly reasonable sentence would offend a specific client. Judgement, taste, relationships and anything that makes a claim on the client's behalf stay with people.

The rules we work by

  1. Every output is reviewed by a person before it reaches a client or the public.
  2. Agents get narrow jobs with clear inputs, never open-ended goals.
  3. Every fact an agent produces must come with a source we can check.
  4. We measure agents on time saved and errors caught, and switch off the ones that do not pay back.

Used this way, agents let a focused team produce the volume of a much larger one without lowering the bar. Used carelessly, they produce a lot of confident noise. The difference is almost entirely in how the work is designed.
