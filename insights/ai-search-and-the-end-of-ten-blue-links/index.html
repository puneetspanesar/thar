AI search and the end of ten blue links

AI answers now sit above the results for a large share of B2B questions. Here is what that does to your traffic, and what to do about it.

For twenty years, search worked the same way. Someone typed a question, Google showed ten links and the best page got the click. That bargain is breaking. AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Copilot now answer many questions directly, and the buyer often never visits a website at all.

For B2B brands, the effect shows up first on informational queries: the "what is", "how to" and "best way to" searches that used to fill the top of the funnel. Rankings can hold steady while clicks fall, because the answer has already been given on the results page.

What actually changes

The goal moves from ranking to being cited. AI systems assemble answers from sources they trust, and they tend to name a small number of brands. If you are one of them, you get the credibility and often the click from buyers who want to go deeper. If you are not, you are invisible for that question.

Commercial searches change more slowly. People comparing vendors, checking prices or looking for a specific company still click through. That is where traffic holds up, and where your site has to work hardest.

Five things we do for every client

  1. Make the brand unmistakable. Consistent names, descriptions and facts about the company across your site, profiles and directories, so AI systems know exactly who you are.
  2. Publish things only you can say. Original data, real examples and clear points of view get cited. Rewritten summaries of other people's articles do not.
  3. Structure every key page. Clear headings, short direct answers near the top, and structured data that tells machines what the page is about.
  4. Win the comparison pages. Build honest pages for the commercial questions buyers ask late in the journey, because those clicks still happen.
  5. Measure differently. Track how often you appear in AI answers for your key questions, alongside rankings, traffic and pipeline.

Where to start

Pick the twenty questions your best customers asked before they bought from you. Ask them to three AI assistants and note who gets mentioned. That list is your real competitive set for the next few years, and it is usually not the one in your sales deck.
